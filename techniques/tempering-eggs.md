# Tempering Eggs

Tempering is used when eggs need to be incorporated into a hot liquid or sauce without scrambling. The technique raises the egg temperature gradually before it meets the full heat.

## How to temper

1. Beat the eggs in a bowl.
2. While whisking constantly, ladle a small amount of the hot liquid (e.g. pasta cooking water, hot cream) into the eggs — a few tablespoons at a time.
3. Once the eggs are warmed up (they won't curdle at the pan's temperature), add them to the main pot or bowl and continue as directed.

## Why it works

Eggs begin to scramble around 70–80°C. Adding hot liquid incrementally raises the egg temperature slowly enough that the proteins don't seize before they're combined with the rest of the sauce.
