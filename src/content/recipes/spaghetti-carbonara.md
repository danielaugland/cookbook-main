## Method

1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil.
2. Heat the olive oil in a large frying pan over medium heat. Add the pancetta and cook slowly until crispy. Add the garlic and cook for 1 minute more. Turn off the heat and transfer the pancetta and garlic to a large bowl.
3. Beat the eggs in a small bowl and mix in half the grated cheese.
4. Cook the pasta in the boiling water until al dente.
5. Using tongs, transfer the pasta directly from the pot to the bowl with the pancetta — let it be dripping wet. Reserve at least 250ml of pasta cooking water.
6. Toss to combine and let the pasta cool for a minute so it won't curdle the eggs.
7. Pour in the egg-and-cheese mixture and toss quickly to coat. Add splashes of reserved pasta water as needed to make the sauce silky and glossy.
8. Season with salt and plenty of black pepper. Serve immediately, topped with the remaining cheese.

## Notes

- Guanciale (cured pork jowl) is the traditional choice; pancetta and bacon are widely used substitutes.
- Garlic is not traditional in Roman carbonara but tastes good — omit if preferred.
- Speed and heat control are everything: the pasta must be hot enough to thicken the eggs but not so hot it scrambles them. Whisk a few tablespoons of hot pasta water into the beaten eggs before adding them to the pasta to reduce the risk of curdling.
